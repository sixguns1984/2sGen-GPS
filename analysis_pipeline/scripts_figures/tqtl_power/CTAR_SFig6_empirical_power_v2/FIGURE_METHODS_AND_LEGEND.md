## Methods

To quantify statistical power among temporal cis-eQTLs identified using CTAR-MPTGA, we randomly selected 5,000 significant SNP–gene pairs from the PPMI discovery dataset (overall temporal genetic-trajectory BH-FDR < 0.05). We matched these pairs to the PDBP CTAR replication results by variant and Ensembl gene identifiers. Power was estimated for the **overall 8-degree-of-freedom likelihood-ratio test**, without separating temporal associations into genetic-effect subclasses. The PPMI discovery test used the empirical raw-P cutoff corresponding to the complete BH-selected set; PDBP replication used P < 0.05. We approximated the distribution of each CTAR likelihood-ratio statistic by a noncentral chi-squared distribution and estimated locus-specific noncentrality with an empirical-Bayes model. A regularized distribution of signal strengths was estimated from the PDBP LRT statistics for PPMI-discovered SNP–gene pairs without selecting on PDBP significance. For PPMI, likelihood inference was conditioned on passage through the discovery cutoff to reduce winner's-curse inflation. Assuming similar genetic effects and per-participant longitudinal information across the two cohorts, the participant-count ratio was used to transfer noncentrality between cohorts. We calculated posterior predictive power separately for discovery and replication and evaluated the proportion of matched pairs with estimated power ≥80%. The probability of passing both tests was approximated by integrating the product of cohort-conditional powers over the posterior noncentrality distribution. Confidence intervals for the proportion of well-powered loci were obtained by gene-cluster bootstrap. To assess sensitivity to sample size, the effective number of participants was scaled from 25% to 100% of the observed cohort size while holding the longitudinal visit-pattern distribution and other assumptions fixed. We additionally repeated the analysis using observation-count scaling. This approach provides a summary-statistic-based power approximation rather than a full participant-bootstrap with repeated CTAR profile-likelihood optimization.

## Figure legend

**Supplementary Fig. 6. Estimated statistical power of observed temporal cis-eQTLs in PPMI and PDBP.** **a**, Illustrative posterior predictive distributions of the overall CTAR 8-df likelihood-ratio statistic for two PPMI temporal cis-eQTLs, with the empirical PPMI discovery significance cutoff marked by a dashed line. **b**, Distribution of model-estimated PPMI discovery power across sampled temporal cis-eQTLs with matched PDBP tests; the proportion with ≥80% power is indicated. **c**, Distribution of model-estimated independent PDBP replication power at nominal P < 0.05 for the same matched pairs. **d**, Proportion of the matched temporal cis-eQTLs with estimated power ≥80% under participant-sample-size fractions of the current cohorts. **e**, Comparison of locus-level estimated discovery and replication power, with dashed lines denoting 80%. **f**, Numbers and proportions of matched temporal cis-eQTLs with ≥80% discovery, replication, or joint statistical power. Estimates use the observed cohort-level CTAR overall test statistics with a selection-aware empirical-Bayes noncentral chi-squared approximation; results should not be interpreted as direct resampling-based empirical power.

## Reporting checklist

- Overall tQTL BH-FDR <0.05: full PPMI count and the raw P-value cutoff.
- 5,000 sampled PPMI pairs: count with PDBP matched overall tests and missing fraction.
- For each fraction ≥80%, report explicit numerator/denominator and gene-cluster bootstrap 95% CI.
- Report actual PDBP nominal replication count and rate separately; a replication rate is not a power estimate.
- Sensitivity to cohort information scaling: independent-participant ratio vs RNA-observation-count ratio.
- Explain that model-based post-selection EB power is a conditional approximation. A subset of loci can be validated by full profile-refit donor-level simulations if computational resources permit.
