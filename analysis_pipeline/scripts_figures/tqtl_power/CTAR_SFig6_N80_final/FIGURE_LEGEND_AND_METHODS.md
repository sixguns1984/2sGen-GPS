**Supplementary Fig. 6 | Prospective sample-size requirements for CTAR-MPTGA temporal tQTL discovery and independent replication.**
**a**, Proportion of sampled SNP-gene pairs attaining at least 80% joint PPMI discovery and PDBP replication power across increasing independent participants per cohort, shown for prospective full-trajectory genetic PVE scenarios of 0.25–4% at MAF 0.20. The horizontal dashed line marks the 80% locus-coverage target; vertical dotted lines indicate the actual discovery and replication cohort sizes. **b**, Minimum equal cohort size (N80/80) necessary for at least 80% of sampled loci to individually attain at least 80% joint temporal-tQTL power. **c**, Sensitivity of N80/80 to planned number of longitudinal observations and the within-participant annual OU correlation (rho), with a common observation horizon and missed follow-up visits. **d**, N80/80 by observed PPMI minor allele frequency, with locus counts indicated. **e**, N80/80 across assumed between-cohort genetic-effect retention scenarios. **f**, Comparison of overall temporal tQTL and stagewise-confirmed dynamic tQTL joint power. The real PDBP replication proportion among the matched, sampled PPMI discoveries is shown separately when available. All panels reflect specified prospective genetic effects, OU-correlated visits, the empirical PPMI discovery threshold, and nominal replication. These are design-specific power approximations, not estimates of the unconditional power of every true cis tQTL.

**Power and minimum sample-size assessment.** We modeled the eight-parameter cubic genetic trajectory used by CTAR-MPTGA, with two non-reference genotype hard-call channels and continuous-time OU within-participant residual correlation. To avoid selection bias from the significance-conditioned discovery set, we did not extrapolate fitted PPMI genetic effects as the source of prospective power. Instead, at least 1,000 distinct discovered SNP-gene pairs (target, 1,500) provided the observed allele-frequency distribution, while prespecified reference-PVE scenarios defined alternative genetic effect sizes. We calculated noncentral chi-square power for the eight-degree-of-freedom overall genetic contrast and, separately, the joint eight-degree-of-freedom overall and six-degree-of-freedom interaction confirmation tests. For the nested tests, joint power was obtained from the independent static and dynamic chi-square components; discovery and replication cohort powers were multiplied only across independent cohorts. Discovery significance used the empirical genome-wide raw screening threshold associated with BH-FDR <0.05 and the stagewise interaction threshold alpha_II=0.05R/G, while independent replication used nominal P<0.05. We defined N80/80 as the smallest equal number of participants in each cohort at which at least 80% of sampled SNP-gene pairs were individually predicted to have at least 80% joint discovery-and-replication power. We varied residual correlation, numbers of visits, allele frequency, genetic trajectory, replication effect retention and cohort size, with gene-block resampling for sensitivity intervals. The primary calculations use fixed-rho large-sample likelihood-ratio approximations; profile-likelihood Monte Carlo and the original CTAR implementation provide complementary finite-sample checks.
